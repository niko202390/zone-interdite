# Zone Interdite: design document

This is a living document. Historical research that supports it lives in `docs/research/`. Asset conventions live in `docs/ASSET_PIPELINE.md`. The ten-mission campaign and its story are in `docs/CAMPAIGN.md`, and the full design of Mission 1 is in `docs/MISSION_01.md`.

## Pitch

It's winter 1943–44 in occupied Brittany. You're a French-Canadian agent of SOE's F Section. Your circuit has been blown and you're stranded inside the coastal *zone interdite*, the restricted military strip along the Atlantic Wall. You have a forged identity card, a coat, and almost nothing else.

London wants one more thing before they'll get you out: German Enigma key material. Steal it, ideally without the Germans ever realising it was taken. Then cross the countryside at night to the cliffs for a Royal Navy pickup.

The game is a slow, hard, quasi-horror stealth/survival FPS. Its tension comes from systems, not scripts. Mission 1 is the only mission planned for now and should take an hour or more.

## Pillars

1. **Slow and emergent.** The length comes from tension, patience and improvising, not from content volume. Every system talks to every other system.
2. **Grounded in history.** Each system mirrors a real wartime mechanism: papers, curfew, telephone exchanges, radio direction-finding, dogs, posters, informants. When the history is uncertain, we go with whatever is plausible and interesting, and mark it in the research notes.
3. **Fear through vulnerability.** You're one person who is under-armed, cold and hunted. Noise is the enemy and a firefight is a failure state you can sometimes survive.
4. **Almost no UI.** Information is diegetic. You read the time on your watch, the route on a paper map, and your ammo by checking the magazine. Health shows in breathing, vision and sound.
5. **Faces stay in shadow.** It's always dusk or night. Helmet brims, cap peaks, scarves and lamp light hide faces, so people
   are silhouettes, postures and voices, never close-ups. This is good for the horror, good for the realism, and good for us
   as a team, since convincing faces are the hardest thing in real-time art.
6. **Made from scratch.** Every model, texture and sound is generated by our own tools (Blender scripts and numpy synthesis). The art target is *immersive*, not full photoreal: dusk, fog, rain and lamplight carry the look.

## Setting

- **Place:** the north coast of Brittany in the Côtes-du-Nord département, as it was called then. It's granite villages, slate roofs, sunken lanes between banked hedgerows, gorse heath and cliffs over the Channel. The towns and the map are fictional but modelled on the real area.
- **Time:** February 1944, on a moonless night. Sunset is early, so dusk turns into a long night. Curfew falls during the mission. The coastal strip needs a special pass and is fortified, mined and patrolled.
- **Model for the extraction:** the Shelburne escape line. From late January 1944, Royal Navy Motor Gun Boats picked people up from Plage Bonaparte below the cliffs at Plouha. The line was run by two French-Canadians, Lucien Dumais and Raymond LaBrosse. Pickups happened on moonless nights after a BBC code phrase, down a mined cliff path, and evaders walked in streams to throw off dogs. Our network and contacts are fictional analogues; real people aren't depicted.

## Core systems

### Body and movement
- Walk, jog and sprint, plus crouch, prone and lean. Stamina, breathing, and footstep loudness that depends on surface and speed.
- Later: wounds and bleeding, being cold and wet, fatigue, and hunger.

### Gunplay
- Hitscan, CS:GO-style. Accuracy is best on the first shot. Moving, stance, fatigue and wounds all add spread. Each weapon has its own recoil pattern.
- Weapons are period-accurate and come in variants and conditions. Manufacturer and production era affect finish and reliability, and worn guns jam or misfire.
- Ammo is tracked by calibre and quality. Several guns share 9 mm Parabellum, which makes scavenging interesting.
- There's no ammo counter. You check the magazine and count your shots.

### Hearing
- Every sound has a physical loudness. Gunshots near the player cause a temporary hearing loss (the world is muffled and a tinnitus tone plays). Repeated exposure, especially indoors, adds a small permanent loss.
- Gameplay cost: a half-deaf player can't hear footsteps, dogs or shouts, so loud play makes stealth harder.

### Stealth and perception
- AI vision depends on light level, distance, movement, silhouette, clothing and posture. AI hearing uses sound propagation and occlusion.
- Suspicion builds through stages: *curious*, *suspicious*, *searching*, *alerted*.
- Social stealth: your cover identity, papers checks, curfew, behaviour that's out of place, and French that may not hold up under scrutiny. In a documented case from June 1943, Germans at a checkpoint grew suspicious of two Canadian agents whose French was "not of native speaker quality".
- AI notices evidence, such as bodies, blood, open doors, cut wires and missing sentries.

### The alert network (our "wanted level")
- Alerts are **information objects** with a position, time and description of you, plus the evidence behind them. They spread through real channels:
  - shouting and whistles
  - field telephones and the PTT exchanges, which only work while lines are intact and exchanges are staffed
  - radio
  - dispatch riders
- Responses escalate from patrols, to search parties with dogs, to cordons and roadblocks, then posters with your description and tighter curfew, and finally radio direction-finding if you transmit.
- **Checkpoints are linked.** Getting past one doesn't mean you're clear. Two agents who escaped a checkpoint at Dhuizon in June 1943 were caught at the next one, about 10 km on.
- **Radio direction-finding comes in three stages.** Fixed stations narrow the area to about 16 km within minutes. Disguised vans sweep that area. Then a man with a detector under his coat walks the last 400 m. The detector can't tell front from back, so he can start off the wrong way, which gives you a window.
- **Curfew is a punishment lever.** In September 1941, Brest's curfew was moved from 23:00–05:00 to 20:00–06:00 over the residents' "rebellious attitude". Loud play makes the night shorter and more dangerous.
- You fight back by cutting wires, silencing the messenger, changing your appearance or going to ground. Heat decays over time but leaves long-term changes (more patrols, warier civilians).

### Civilians
- Schedules and dispositions: Resistance, neutral, collaborator or informant. Civilians feel fear, can report you, can shelter you, or can sell you out.
- Contacts and safehouses are part of the mission's approaches.

### Survival and camp (the hideout)
**Status:** designed, not built. Planned for Mission 3 ("Safe House"). Mission 1 only has safehouse save points.

**Rule:** every hideout activity must feed the missions. Nothing is there for its own sake.

| Activity | What it's for |
|---|---|
| Sleep | The only way to save. Also heals light wounds and restores stamina. |
| Fire and drying clothes | Being cold and wet slows you, makes you shiver (aim sway), and makes you cough (noise). A fire fixes that, but its smoke and light can be seen from outside. |
| Forging desk | Turn stolen blanks, stamps and photos into papers. Papers open approaches, like the zone pass in the test level. |
| Intel board | Pin leads from your notebook. Linking two leads reveals an approach, such as courier + café = the steamed envelope. |
| Wireless set | Listen to the BBC, and transmit to arrange drops. Transmitting brings the radio-detection vans (see the alert network). |
| Cache | Store what you can't carry. Choose your loadout per mission, since pockets are small. |
| Contacts | People who visit or need visiting. Their trust opens routes and items. |

### Mission 1: the pinch
- At least four distinct approaches to the Enigma material, in the Hitman style. Some examples:
  - a cover job inside, such as delivering laundry or food or working as a PTT lineman
  - a night break-in by roof or cellar
  - a diversion such as a cut phone line or a power failure
  - intercepting the courier who carries the key sheets
  - turning or blackmailing a clerk
- **The target is the key sheet, not just the machine.** Each day's strip was cut off and destroyed after use. Kriegsmarine keys were printed in red water-soluble ink on pink paper, so a swim through the surf can ruin your prize.
- The outcomes are graded. The best is **"they never knew"**: the material is photographed or swapped, so the Germans don't change their keys. Worse is "stolen but covered up", then "stolen and they know". The Allies really did go to great lengths to hide their captures; *Lauenburg* and *U-110* were both sunk afterwards so the Germans wouldn't find out.
- The escape runs through the countryside and into the coastal zone, ending in a pickup on a moonless night at the cliffs.

### Audio and voice
- VO barks and dialogue in German, French and English. How we produce the voices is still undecided (see Open questions).
- Synthesized ambience covers wind, rain, sea, church bells and distant engines. Sparse horror music cues come in at moments of dread. Alarms, dogs and shouted orders are the terror moments.

## History and the liberties we take

Details are in `docs/research/german-security-and-enigma.md`, including a scorecard of what the research confirmed and what it didn't.

- **The heist is fiction.** No documented case was found of agents taking Enigma material on land in France. Our mission is a plausible "what if" built on real procedure.
- **Germans cutting mains power block by block to locate a transmitter:** unverified. If we use it, it's a deliberate liberty.
- **Dropped:** Fieseler Storch spotter planes hunting fugitives. That was mostly an Eastern Front practice.
- **The Milice** only extended into the north on 27 January 1944, and its presence in Brittany is thin until summer. Use the Feldgendarmerie, Geheime Feldpolizei, SD and Vichy gendarmes instead. Use the Breton SD auxiliaries (Bezen Perrot) sparingly and carefully, if at all.
- **Plan Violet**, the mass cutting of telephone lines, came in June 1944, after our window. In February 1944, line cutting is individual sabotage, and the PTT Resistance mostly tapped lines.

## Open questions

- **Voice acting:** synthesize it ourselves, use a local open-source TTS, use a TTS service, or use human actors.
- Protagonist name and backstory.
- Whether to show German and Vichy insignia, and how. This is about historical accuracy versus German display law for released builds; it's moot while the game is private.
- Exact Enigma installation, e.g. a Kriegsmarine signals post in a requisitioned manor near a small port. This waits on the research.
